Name lists

Each culture's file holds its lists, each under a line "[<list>]", with a name and how many people
have it on each line, most common first.
hammer-plugins' real-names/tools/ builds them, a script for each culture.

american.txt

feminine, masculine, and the lists by decade of birth hold the 1,000 most common given names
of each sex among Social Security card applications for births in the United States, over 1880 to
the latest year published and within each decade. From the Social Security Administration's national
baby names data, https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html. It gives names without spaces,
hyphens, or capitals inside them. A name over 20 times as common for the other sex is left out.

surnames holds the 5,000 most common surnames of the 2010 census. From the Census Bureau's
Frequently Occurring Surnames from the 2010 Census,
https://www.census.gov/topics/population/genealogy/data/2010_surnames.html. It gives surnames in
capitals without spaces or punctuation; the script restores those of the common ones.

Both are works of the United States government, in the public domain.

chinese.txt

The lists hold the most common given names of each sex among those born in China from 1930 to 2008,
over all those years and for each cohort of birth (1930 to 1959, under 1950, then each decade), and
the surnames, in pinyin without tones, names of the same pinyin counted as one. From the ChineseNames
package's database, which the National Citizen Identity Information Center gathered in 2008:

  Bao, H.-W.-S. (2023). ChineseNames: Chinese Name Database 1930-2008. R package,
  https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=ChineseNames, https://github.com/psychbruce/ChineseNames.

Unlike the rest of this plugin, these lists are shared under their source's terms: the GNU GPL
version 3 (licenses/GPL-3.0.txt) and the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike
licence, of which the source names no version (licenses/CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0.txt is version 4.0). They
may be used for non-commercial purposes only, and shared only under the same terms.

argentine.txt

feminine, masculine, and the lists by decade of birth from the 1920s to the 2010s hold the 1,000 most
common whole given names of each sex among the people RENAPER, Argentina's national register of
persons, has registered as born from 1922 to 2015, over all those years and within each decade, and
the lists for the 2020s the 1,000 most common single given names of each sex among those born from
2020 to 2024, a person with two names counted for each. surnames holds the 5,000 most common surnames
of Argentina's residents in December 2021, of those more than 10 of a province's residents have. From
RENAPER's Nombres de personas físicas,
https://infra.datos.gob.ar/catalog/otros/dataset/2/distribution/2.1/download/historico-nombres.zip,
and its Nombres propios en Argentina and Distribución de apellidos en Argentina,
https://datos.gob.ar/dataset/nombres-propios-en-argentina1 and
https://datos.gob.ar/dataset/distribucion-de-apellidos-en-argentina1, shared under CC BY 4.0,
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. RENAPER gives no sex for a name; the script tells it
from the name's words, by Wikidata's Argentine people and RENAPER's most common names of each sex, and
leaves out the few names whose words tell none. It restores the accents RENAPER's records leave off.
The lists by decade before the 1920s are from Wikidata, below.

under CC BY 4.0. A name over 20 times as common for the other sex is left out. surnames and the lists

brazilian.txt

feminine, masculine, and the lists by decade of birth since the 1930s hold the 1,000 most common
first given names of each sex among Brazil's residents on 1 August 2022, over all of them and by
decade of birth, the 1930s list holding everyone born before 1940, and surnames the 5,000 most common
surnames, each counted wherever it is among a person's surnames. From IBGE's Nomes no Brasil, from the
2022 census, https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/nomes/, through its API,
https://servicodados.ibge.gov.br/api/v3/nomes/2022. It gives only names 20 or more people have, in
small letters without accents; the script restores those of the common ones. It gives a decade's names
for both sexes together, so a decade's split by sex is the name's split over all years. A name over 20
times as common for the other sex is left out, as is a name a hundred times rarer than one a letter
away from it, taken as a slip in writing that one. Also left out are a surname more people have as
their first given name (most often a second given name the census counts among surnames), a given
name a hundred times as many have as a surname, and words that are answers, particles, or
abbreviations rather than surnames. IBGE states no licence for these lists; they are shared openly
and used here with IBGE named as their source. The masculine lists for decades before the 1930s are
from Wikidata, below.

canadian.txt

feminine, masculine, and the lists by decade of birth since the 1990s hold the 1,000 most common
given names of each sex among births in Canada from 1991 to 2025, and within each decade. From
Statistics Canada's table 17-10-0147-01, First names at birth by sex at birth, selected indicators,
https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1710014701, which gives names 5 or more were
given in a year. Adapted from Statistics Canada, First names at birth by sex at birth, selected
indicators, 1991 to 2025; this does not constitute an endorsement by Statistics Canada of this
product. Shared under the Statistics Canada Open Licence,
https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/terms-conditions/open-licence. The lists for the 1980s add the births of
Quebec (below), Ontario, Alberta, and British Columbia; those for the 1920s to the 1970s hold English
Canada's, the births of Ontario and British Columbia only. From Ontario's top baby names,
https://data.ontario.ca, names 5 or more were given in a year, which contains information licensed
under the Open Government Licence – Ontario, https://www.ontario.ca/page/open-government-licence-ontario;
British Columbia's most popular names for the past 100 years, https://www2.gov.bc.ca, which contains
information licensed under the Open Government Licence – British Columbia,
https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content?id=A519A56BC2BF44E4A008B33FCF527F61; and Alberta's frequency and
ranking of baby names, https://open.alberta.ca, which contains information licensed under the Open
Government Licence – Alberta, https://open.alberta.ca/licence. Ontario's Marie and Joseph, often a
French Canadian's first given name and not the one used, are counted as often as in British Columbia.
The sources write names in capitals without accents; the script writes a French name as Quebec
does, and one of English too as most of those who have it do. A name over 20 times as common for
the other sex is left out. surnames and the lists for earlier decades are from Wikidata, below.

israeli.txt, levantine.txt

feminine, masculine, and the lists by decade of birth hold the 1,000 most common first given names of
each sex in Israel's population register in April 2025 among those born from 1949 to 2024, over all
those years and within each decade, the 1940s being 1949 alone; surnames holds the most common
surnames of 2017. israeli.txt holds those of the Jewish sector, and levantine.txt those of the Muslim,
Christian-Arab, and Druze sectors together. From the Central Bureau of Statistics' The Current Names of
Those Born 1949-2024 (release 391/2025),
https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2025/391/11_25_391t1.xlsx, and Last Names in Israel 2017

from a table it keeps; names without a sure spelling are left out. A name is in the given names when 5
or more people of its sex have it, in a year's count when 5 or more born that year have it, and in the
surnames when more than 30 people have it. A name over 20 times as common for the other sex is left
out. The lists of masculine names by decade before the 1940s in israeli.txt are from Wikidata, below.

mexican.txt

feminine-2010 and masculine-2010 hold the most common given names of each sex among births
registered in Mexico from 2017 to 2019, those given 745 times or more, and feminine-2020 and
masculine-2020 those among births registered in 2020 and 2021, each name as registered, such as

keeps them, https://web.archive.org. They give names in capitals without accents; the script restores
those of the names Mexico's people on Wikidata have and of the common ones, and counts a surname whose
Ñ a record lost or doubled under its Ñ's. A name over 20 times as common for the other sex is left out.
Fuente: INEGI. Shared under INEGI's Términos de Libre Uso de la Información del INEGI,
https://www.inegi.org.mx/inegi/terminos.html, which allow copying, adapting, and commercial use with
the credit "Fuente: INEGI" and a note of any changes made. The other lists are from Wikidata, below.

danish.txt, dutch.txt, filipino.txt, greek.txt, hungarian.txt, indian.txt, iranian.txt,
italian.txt, japanese.txt, korean.txt, nigerian.txt, russian.txt, turkish.txt, ukrainian.txt, and
what the above lack

The lists hold the first given names and the family names of each country's people born since 1880
that Wikidata, https://www.wikidata.org, has, by their English labels or, where a name has none, its
label for all languages, and how many of those people have each: names at least 3 have, and a decade's
list where it has at least 500 people and 50 names. A country's people are its citizens, and those of
the states before it born where it is now; England's are the United Kingdom's, and Quebec's are
Canada's citizens born in Quebec. Japanese names are without the marks on their letters. Czech, Greek,
Russian, and Ukrainian surnames are counted for both sexes together, and a woman's formed from a man's
by its ending, or as women have it where they do. They are the names of people of note, so follow the
population's only roughly. Wikidata's data is in the public domain (CC0).

egyptian.txt, maghrebi.txt

The lists hold the first given names and the family names of Egypt's people (egyptian.txt), and of
Morocco's, Algeria's, and Tunisia's together (maghrebi.txt), born since 1880 that Wikidata,
https://www.wikidata.org, has, and how many of those people have each: names at least 3 have, and a
decade's list where it has at least 500 people and 50 names. A country's people are its citizens, and
those of the states before it born where it is now (for Egypt the Khedivate, Sultanate, Kingdom, and
Republic of Egypt, the United Arab Republic, and the Ottoman Empire; for Tunisia the Beylik of Tunis,
the French protectorate, and the Ottoman Empire), each counted once; France's citizens born in the
Maghreb are not. Each name is spelled as the person's own English label spells it, as Wikidata often
links a Mohamed to the name Muhammad; a name the label has in none of its spellings counts for no one,
and the marks of a scholar's transliteration, as in Muḥammad, are left off. They are the names of
people of note, so follow the population's only roughly. Wikidata's data is in the public domain
(CC0).
